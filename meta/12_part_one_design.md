# 12 — Part One Design

This file analyzes a specific structural question raised for this consolidation. **It does not implement anything.** No chapter has been rewritten, resequenced, or restructured as a result of this file. It exists to hold the analysis and the leading option in one place so a future drafting decision can be made deliberately rather than by default.

## The Question Under Analysis [OPEN QUESTION — tracked in `16_open_questions.md`]

**Should Noor remain present, on the page, for longer than the current plan allows?**

Current committed structure, per `meta/chapter_outline`: Noor appears as a fully embodied, living human being only in Chapter One ("The Metal Stick"). She leaves for the access review at the end of that chapter and does not return to the page as a living presence — Part One's remaining chapters (Two through Seven, per the outline) track B living around her negative space, with her Foam-presence becoming legible only gradually and never as direct on-page interaction.

## The Leading Structural Principle Under Consideration [CANDIDATE IDEA — not yet adopted]

> The reader should remember more of Noor than corrected-B does.

This is a deliberately asymmetric reading experience: B's grief was attenuated into functionality (`08_characters.md` §B, `03_correction.md`), meaning he has *lost access* to large parts of the relational structure that made Noor matter to him — while the reader, having witnessed Chapter One directly, retains all of it. The gap between what the reader remembers and what B can access becomes a structural engine for dramatic irony throughout Part One: the reader recognizes echoes of Noor (the oranges, the toaster, the air conditioner's grief-without-source) that B cannot fully place, not because he's obtuse, but because the relevant connective tissue has been institutionally removed from him.

**Why this matters for the "Noor present longer" question:** if Chapter One is the reader's *only* direct exposure to living Noor, the density of that single chapter has to carry the entire weight of "the reader should remember more of Noor than B does" for the rest of Part One (six more chapters, per the outline) and arguably the rest of the book. That is a lot of narrative weight for one chapter to bear alone.

## Arguments For Extending Noor's On-Page Presence [CANDIDATE IDEA, analysis only]

- A single chapter of lived relationship, however dense, may not generate enough specific, concrete texture (jokes, routines, disagreements, small tendernesses) for her absence to register as *specific* rather than *generic* grief for the remainder of Part One.
- The asymmetric-memory principle above works better the more the reader has to remember. More on-page Noor = a wider, more textured gap between reader-knowledge and B's-accessible-knowledge = more opportunities for dramatic irony without exposition.
- `meta/style_guide`'s guardrail against "every Bloomrot manifestation carrying a message intended for B" is easier to honor if the reader has enough independent memory of Noor to recognize an echo *themselves*, without needing B or the narration to point at it.

## Arguments Against Extending Noor's On-Page Presence [CANDIDATE IDEA, analysis only]

- Chapter One is explicitly structured (per `meta/chapter_outline`'s own stated meaning) as "the only chapter in which Noor appears as a fully embodied, living human being" — this scarcity may be load-bearing for the reader's felt sense of loss. A single, complete, dense chapter that the reader can never return to may generate more grief than a slower fade across multiple chapters would.
- The existing five-movement architecture (`meta/novel_overview`) is built around B living around Noor's negative space beginning in Chapter Two ("Present day," five years later). Inserting more living-Noor chapters would require either a flashback structure not currently planned, or delaying the five-year time jump — both are structural changes with wide ripple effects into Parts Two through Five.
- Chapter One is already extremely dense with Noor characterization (per the current draft: the orange-cutting opening, the rice-ball exchange, the Kyoto concierge story, "Fruit is intimacy," the full case-comparison sequence, the departure). It is not clear the chapter is currently under-serving her.

## What This File Recommends [NARRATIVE INTENT — a recommendation, not a decision]

Do not resolve this question by silently drafting more Noor chapters, and do not resolve it by silently declaring Chapter One sufficient. This is exactly the kind of question the README's source-of-truth hierarchy exists to protect: it is a live structural fork with real costs on both sides, and it should be decided deliberately — ideally by drafting a candidate insertion point (e.g., a second flashback chapter placed non-adjacently within Part One) and testing whether it earns its place, rather than by assumption in either direction.
