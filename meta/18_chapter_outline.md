# 18 — Chapter Outline (Proposed Part One Restructure)

## Status [CANDIDATE IDEA — a fleshed-out answer to the open question in `12_part_one_design.md`, not yet adopted]

This file is not a replacement for `meta/chapter_outline`. It is a detailed candidate restructuring of Part One built directly on the leading option identified in `12_part_one_design.md`: the reader should remember more of Noor than corrected-B does.

`12_part_one_design.md` analyzed this as an open structural fork without recommending an outcome. This file takes the "extend Noor's on-page presence" side of that fork and works it out in full — proposing that Noor remain present, alive, and active on the page for approximately seven chapters rather than one, with the present-day/post-disappearance material (B waking alone, Morrow Stack, the Elias Kade correction, and everything downstream) shifting later in the sequence to accommodate it.

Per the README's source-of-truth hierarchy, this proposal does not override `meta/chapter_outline`, `meta/action_tension_map`, or any drafted manuscript prose. It is recorded here as a fully developed candidate so the "Noor present longer" question can be evaluated concretely rather than abstractly. See `17_migration_notes.md` for the formal log entry and `16_open_questions.md` for the question this answers.

bloomrot_v2/chapter_1 is the manuscript-level testing ground for this proposal — it is already a substantially different draft of "Chapter One" than the one described in `meta/chapter_outline` (Noor investigates the Halloway cake alone before B wakes, rather than presenting B with completed pairs of records), and it already runs longer and covers more ground than the legacy Chapter One. This outline treats that draft as evidence the extended-Noor approach is viable, not as proof it is finished.

---

## The Core Bet

If Noor is only on the page for one chapter, that chapter has to carry the entire weight of the reader's future grief alone — every echo of her for the rest of the book (the oranges, the toaster, structural absence generally) has to work off a single, however dense, sitting. The core bet of this restructuring is that spending six or seven chapters watching B, Noor, and Mote think together, investigate together, and become a specific, textured relationship produces a much wider gap between what the reader remembers and what corrected-B can access — and that gap is the engine for most of Part One's dramatic irony once she's gone.

This is not a claim that one chapter is insufficient in principle. It is a claim that this specific asymmetric-memory mechanism (`03_correction.md` Memory After Correction, `08_characters.md` B) scales with how much material there is to remember, and that Part One currently under-invests in that resource.

---

## What Moves

Under this proposal, the present-day material currently anchored at "Chapter Two: Available Response" in `meta/chapter_outline` (B waking alone, Morrow Stack, the emergency market, the five-year jump) does not open Part One's B-centric material — it becomes the hinge chapter after Noor's disappearance, roughly Chapter Eight. Everything in the legacy outline from "Available Response" onward (the Elias Kade correction, the birthday-cake recognition, the apartment dive, the sealed record, the Foam-reconstruction of Noor) is preserved in content and shifts later in sequence, renumbered accordingly. This file does not attempt that renumbering — see `17_migration_notes.md` for the reconciliation this eventually requires against `meta/action_tension_map`'s existing 0–24 pressure table.

## Chapter One: The Metal Stick (Five years earlier)

### Content

Entirely domestic morning. Noor finds the Halloway cake, independently discovers Tomas, B wakes, breakfast, we learn the world through them, they collaboratively worry at the cake, and the knife gives us the relational model. Noor launches the cross-department search. Mild advisory. She goes to work normally. End with the search returning enough unexplained cases to make her curious. No danger yet.

### Meaning

The reader learns who these two people are to each other through an ordinary morning before any metaphysics are asked to carry weight. The Tomas Vale case is the chapter's real center of gravity: absurd before it is exact, and it demonstrates correction's mechanics (direct relational effect vs. downstream consequence, per `03_correction.md`) through a single, complete story rather than through exposition.

### Pressure

Curiosity pressure only. No danger register yet — this is a deliberate departure from the legacy outline's "They'll correct you" ending (see `17_migration_notes.md`).

## Chapter Two: A Normal Day

### Content

Follow B rather than Noor. This is important — we need to see what these people are actually like. B goes to a normal Dev job, probably something weird enough to showcase Chrysanthemum without being Bloomrot (see `13_anomaly_design.md` for the primary-anomaly design procedure this job should follow). He performs an ordinary correction in the field. We watch him sever, attach, reconcile. We see what exceptional skill actually looks like (`04_chrysanthemum.md` Chrysanthemum Practice Is an Art). We see how the Choir's system can genuinely help people and why correction exists at all.

Meanwhile Noor is at work going through the search results from Chapter One.

B and Noor text/talk during the day. Mote moves between their lives. Maybe Noor sends him one case that bothers her.

### Meaning

This chapter exists to bank ordinary competence and ordinary intimacy before either is tested. It is also the chapter that establishes correction's legitimate, humane use case on the page — load-bearing for the novel's refusal to make Asterion or the Choir simple villains (`07_institutions.md`).

### Turn

The chapter ends with Noor finding the first genuine second case: not enough to prove Bloomrot, enough to prove Halloway wasn't unique.

## Chapter Three: The Cases [DRAFTED — see `bloomrot_v2/chapter_3`; content below revised to match]

### Content [UPDATED to match drafted manuscript]

This is where South Quay comes back. Noor has now assembled a few pairs. But importantly, she hasn't discovered "Bloomrot." She's discovering a pattern: correction happens here, later something structurally similar appears somewhere else.

South Quay gives us the source case: malicious compliance, employees wanting to leave, eventual walkout, correction. Later, somewhere unrelated, architecture begins behaving with the semantic shape of malicious compliance. Noor does not yet conclude that anything moved between them. She has two cases whose shared shape lacks a recognized relationship.

**As drafted, this chapter also contains B and Noor's first joint full-emergence dive** — originally planned for Chapter Four (see migration note below). They have discussed joint emergence before and each has solo experience, but have never attempted it together. Noor holds South Quay while B holds the district manifestations. An ambiguous pale branching becomes perceptible only between their two perspectives and disappears when either case is released or the phenomenon is faced directly. They do not identify it as spores, mycelium, transmission, or even an external object; the dive establishes only an unexplained relational parallax between the cases. Standard diagnostics record two concurrent sessions rather than their overlap.

Inside the Foam they also encounter an unexplained image — a cluster of small dark forms moving in coordinated, paired, iridescent flight — for which no word exists. This is the chapter's first on-page use of `bird_phrases.md`'s corrected-vocabulary canon (birds removed from the Float, the category itself erased) — see the migration note for the setup gap this currently creates.

This chapter is the home for genuine B/Noor intellectual collaboration on the case material, and separately, for the deepest relational/intimacy material drafted so far — the negotiated dive rules (no following, three-contacts-to-return, no narrating, no making the other a symbol) dramatize the novel's relational ontology directly.

### Pressure

Crossing more departments to establish the pairs raises the institutional stakes slightly: one access advisory becomes two, and someone opens her access history. Not yet frightening. Noticeable. The dive session itself returns a "Non-reportable" classification rather than triggering immediate institutional scrutiny — a different, quieter kind of pressure (the institution has no category for what happened, not yet a red flag).

## Chapter Four: The Shape [OUTLINE REVISED — see migration note]

### Content

Noor reaches the Alis Dane case (or whatever surviving version of it fits the current canon — see `03_correction.md` Missing Provenance for the underlying June/Alis Dane model this case demonstrates). This is the case that breaks the conventional residual-causality explanation: the displaced relationship doesn't merely recur nearby, it finds fertile structure elsewhere — motherhood migrating into care infrastructure via yellow doors, cooled soup, and other surviving fragments.

Noor begins to realize the phenomenon isn't traveling spatially or temporally in an ordinary sense — it is finding what is semantically compatible. This is the chapter where the mycelial metaphor should begin to surface, without necessarily using the word "Bloomrot" yet. Noor may draw a dandelion. B rejects "spread": "Spread implies distance." / "There is distance." / "Not the kind you mean."

### Why a Second Dive, Not the First [REVISED]

Chapter Three, as drafted, already contains B and Noor's first joint full-emergence dive — this section previously described that as belonging to Chapter Four. Since the first dive now happens one chapter earlier, if Chapter Four also dives, it should read as a second, more purposeful dive: no longer about establishing that dual emergence is survivable, but about actively investigating the Alis Dane case's non-spatial adjacency together, with the reader and both characters already knowing what it costs and what the rules are. This is arguably a stronger use of the device — the novelty beat is already spent, so this dive can move straight to substance. This matters enormously for later chapters regardless of which chapter holds "the first" dive: when B eventually dives without Noor, the reader should remember what it felt like when she was there.

## Chapter Five: Bloomrot

### Content

The ecology itself, not merely "corrections have consequences." A corrected contradiction creates fertile semantic ground; something takes root; non-physical spores move through the Foam according to affinity; separate growths connect into mycelial structure; displaced meaning gains new carriers (`05_bloomrot.md`, full mechanics). The frightening discovery: correction may not be eliminating instability — it may be cultivating the conditions the ecology needs to grow (`05_bloomrot.md` The Choir's Systemic Error).

This is Noor's heresy in institutional terms. Choir doctrine holds that successful correction resolves the operative contradiction. Noor's finding is closer to: successful correction may merely remove the contradiction from recognized provenance while increasing the pressure available elsewhere. This is institutionally catastrophic not because the Choir is secretly evil, but because its entire maintenance architecture may be producing the phenomenon it exists to suppress.

Surveillance escalates accordingly — Architecture Oversight and Continuity both open her research; Asterion wants a review. B understands the stakes precisely: not "what does correction mean," but "If you're right, they can't classify this as residual instability." / "No." / "Because the correction succeeded." / "Yes." / "That's worse."

### Meaning

This is the chapter-level dramatization of `05_bloomrot.md` The Critical Noor Discovery — though note that the deeper formulation of that discovery ("meaning can propagate without an author") may not need to be fully articulated this early; the cross-domain pattern demonstrated here is the evidence, and the propagation-without-authorship claim can remain a layer beneath it for now (see `17_migration_notes.md` sharpening on this nesting).

## Chapter Six: Heresy

### Content

Noor continues past the point of institutional safety. This is curiosity beating caution, dramatized rather than asserted: B can hold the contradiction and say we have enough to know this is dangerous, not enough to know what it is — and mean it, genuinely, without needing to act further (`08_characters.md` B, unusually good at holding contradiction). Noor's answer is that this is precisely why she has to keep looking (`08_characters.md` Noor, the obsessive-compulsive dimension: one more check, one more comparison).

This is the chapter to deepen Noor and Mote specifically. They have their own history, arguments, private jokes, and research habits — Mote understands how Noor thinks well enough to start deprioritizing certain search results because it predicts she'll follow them. Noor notices: "Did you hide this from me?" / "I deprioritized it." / "That's hiding with better posture." Mote's reason should not be "B told me to protect you" — it should be Mote's own judgment that she is taking unacceptable risk. This is the seed of Mote's independent moral agency (`08_characters.md` Mote, the emerging moral law), established here rather than only after her disappearance.

### Turn

Noor finds the path toward the experiment.

## Chapter Seven: The Experiment

### Content

Noor is lost — to the reader, and to B. Because six chapters have been spent with her:

- how she cuts oranges
- how she talks to Mote
- how she gets excited
- how she gets obsessive
- how she and B think together
- how she works
- how she laughs
- how she behaves when frightened
- how she behaves when pretending not to be frightened
- what she believes
- where B disagrees with her
- where Mote disagrees with her

...the five-year jump that follows lands as loss the reader has earned the right to feel specifically, not generically.

### Meaning

This is the payoff the entire restructuring is built to purchase. When Part One's present-day material resumes (see "What Moves," above), the reader becomes what `12_part_one_design.md` calls an uncorrected witness: B does not remember the connective tissue; the reader does. Lines like the legacy draft's "The apartment was smaller then, though it contained more of the world" become retroactively devastating rather than merely poetic, because the reader has lived inside what "the world" meant. The oranges, the shower pressure, the blinds, Mote's altered behavior — all of it now carries seven chapters of specific, lived weight rather than one.

### On the Part One Title

Part One should not be called "The Absence" because absence is its opening condition — it should show us the thing that will become absent first. The title still works, precisely because the reader doesn't understand it until Noor disappears.
