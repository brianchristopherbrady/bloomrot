## Candidate Ending Images [SERIOUS CANDIDATE — leading option for the novel's final image, not yet drafted]

15. **A single bird lands somewhere B can see it, at the very end of the book, with no explanation offered on the page.** Raised in session as an exploratory alternative to any "B was too certain of the world" backstory (explicitly rejected as inconsistent with `08_characters.md` — B holds contradiction comfortably and does not need to be humbled by an unresolved question; forcing him to have been "too certain" would be an invented flaw, not an earned one). **Upgraded from exploratory to a serious candidate for the book's actual final scene** — this is now the leading idea for how Bloomrot ends, not merely a stray image to place somewhere.

   The candidate reasoning, recorded for whoever next works on the ending: `bird_phrases.md` establishes birds were removed specifically as a category of *unlicensed movement* — migration, flocking, occupying the sky without route authorization — making them a concentrated symbol of everything the Choir's correction apparatus exists to prevent. `10_themes.md` §Provenance and Missingness already establishes the ending cannot be total restoration; the middle path is provenance, not recovery. If B's final act of consequence is a refusal to seal something shut — choosing not to correct, not to close, not to finish an erasure — rather than an act of repair or control, then one unlicensed thing slipping back through the resulting gap is a *consequence* of that refusal, not a reward, a message, or a spell. Nobody "brings back birds." A crack simply stops being closed for one moment, and unlicensed movement does what it does.

   This would also mirror chapter_3's already-drafted "There should be a word." / "There was one. Once." beat (the murmuration during the joint dive) — but inverted onto the reader rather than onto B. In chapter_3, B reaches for a lost word and fails, and the reader shares his failure. In this candidate ending, the reader would recognize the bird and B would not — "What's that?" — reversing which party holds the missing knowledge. This is deliberately left unresolved on the page: B does not learn what it is, and no in-scene explanation should be added.

   **Session decision on supporting setup (2026-08-17):** rather than continuing to plant additional corrected bird-idioms as ambient Choir programming (assessed in-session as already at or past the right density — see `bird_phrases.md`'s single confirmed idiom in chapter_1's ribbon sequence), the chosen setup path going forward is: (a) at most one more idiom or motivational-poster instance somewhere in the manuscript, used sparingly, not stacked; and (b) a dedicated conversation, not yet written, in which a character articulates the Choir's institutional hatred of *unlicensed movement* in the abstract — anything that migrates, flocks, self-organizes, or crosses boundaries without route authorization — without naming birds or gesturing at the missing category at all. The scene should describe the problem birds historically posed to the Choir's coordination model without the word "bird" ever surfacing, so the connection is legible only in hindsight, after the ending image lands. This conversation is not yet drafted and has no assigned chapter.

   Status: leading candidate for the book's actual ending; not yet drafted. Requires a future decision about (a) what B's final refusal actually is, (b) confirming this belongs at the very end of the book rather than elsewhere, (c) whether it survives contact with whatever the actual ending scene needs once drafted, and (d) drafting the unlicensed-movement conversation described above and choosing where it belongs. Logged here specifically so it is not lost, per this file's own instruction not to let ideas be silently resolved or silently forgotten.

## How to Resolve One of These

If a drafting decision forces resolution of any item above (for example, a scene requires committing to whether the mycelium is conscious), do the following:

1. Draft the scene with the resolution it requires.
2. Add an entry to `17_migration_notes.md` recording which question was resolved, what the resolution was, which scene forced it, and the date.
3. Remove the item from this list, or mark it "RESOLVED — see migration notes" rather than deleting the historical record.
4. Update every file in `meta_v2` that referenced the question as open.

Do not resolve an open question implicitly by simply writing a scene that assumes an answer without recording the decision. That is exactly the silent-resolution failure mode this reorganization exists to prevent.
